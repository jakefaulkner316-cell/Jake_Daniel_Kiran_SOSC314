WEEK 5 DICTIONARY AND PREPROCESSING SENSITIVITY DIAGNOSTIC
=================================================================

DIAGNOSTIC QUESTION
-----------------------------------------------------------------
Do the recession-related sentiment findings remain similar when the sentiment dictionary and preprocessing method change?

DIRECTIONAL ROBUSTNESS
-----------------------------------------------------------------
Positive sentiment is lower during recessions under every tested specification: True

Negative sentiment is higher during recessions under every tested specification: False

SPECIFICATION RESULTS
-----------------------------------------------------------------

Dictionary: Harvard
Preprocessing: All Words
Positive recession difference: -0.0291 pp
Negative recession difference: +0.0021 pp

Dictionary: Harvard
Preprocessing: No Stopwords
Positive recession difference: -0.0637 pp
Negative recession difference: -0.0039 pp

Dictionary: Harvard
Preprocessing: Dictionary Only
Positive recession difference: -0.0532 pp
Negative recession difference: +0.1893 pp

Dictionary: Loughran-McDonald
Preprocessing: All Words
Positive recession difference: -0.0753 pp
Negative recession difference: +0.0250 pp

Dictionary: Loughran-McDonald
Preprocessing: No Stopwords
Positive recession difference: -0.1164 pp
Negative recession difference: +0.0323 pp

Dictionary: Loughran-McDonald
Preprocessing: Dictionary Only
Positive recession difference: -1.6527 pp
Negative recession difference: +0.6171 pp

INTERPRETATION
-----------------------------------------------------------------

The positive-sentiment result is directionally robust because every tested combination of dictionary and preprocessing method shows lower positive advertising language during recession quarters.

However, the magnitude of the positive effect changes across specifications, meaning the estimated effect size is sensitive to how sentiment is measured.

Negative sentiment is less stable than positive sentiment. Harvard changes direction depending on preprocessing, while Loughran-McDonald shows higher negative language during recessions across all three preprocessing methods.

The dictionary-only specification produces much larger percentage-point effects because the denominator contains only words recognized by the dictionary. These results are therefore on a different scale from the all-words and no-stopwords specifications.
