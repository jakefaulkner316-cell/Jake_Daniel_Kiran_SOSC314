HOW TO READ THIS
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
- Part 1 asks whether the MIX changes. A sector whose share rises or falls in
  recessions is a candidate composition channel, but only if that sector's
  typical tone differs from others (see mean sentiment by sector).
- Part 2 is the direct test. Model C compares recession and other quarters
  within decades. Model E additionally compares each advertiser only with
  itself. If C and E are similar, composition is not what drives the
  recession difference; if E moves toward zero, it is.
- Model D (sector FE) shows how much of any composition effect is broad
  industry mix, as opposed to individual firms.
- Part 3 gives the same idea as an accounting identity at sector level: the
  'between-sector' term is what the difference would be if every sector kept
  its tone and only the sector mix changed.
- Ad-level estimates weight each ad equally, so they need not equal the
  quarterly averages in scripts 03a-04d, which weight each quarter equally.
- Sector rules are keyword-based and coarse; Part 2's advertiser FE do not
  depend on them. Brand names in the source data are sometimes wrong (see 01b).
- All results are descriptive associations, not causal effects.
